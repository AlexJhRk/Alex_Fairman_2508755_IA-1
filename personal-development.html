7 Cures for a Lean Purse

This chapter started off by showing us that the citizens of the city of Babylon was under extreme poverty. Because of this the King was in concerned deeply about his people and had to find a way to help them. He discovered that there were a few men in the city that were wealthy by a vast comparison. The King was puzzled by how these could have so much money and demanded that a man wise on becoming wealthy spoke to selected men of the city to teach them about wealth so that they too can spread their teachings amongst others.

The first cure

This is a very interesting chapter that is all about the first teachings of the cures to a lean purse to 100 specifically chosen poor men of the city of Babylon. Throughout the chapter, Arkad explains to the men that were gathered that he was once in the same situation as the rest of them. He was once in a deep well of poverty. He started to explain to the men that if every morning they add 10 eggs to a basket and remove 9 every night then eventually it would begin to overflow within time. So each man should only remove 9/10 of their gold for bills like food, clothes and other necessities and eventually their purse will fatten.

The second cure

The second cure for a lean purse which was explained by Arkad was that thy should not confuse necessities with desires and that is what most men were confused about and doing. One man made an argument that choosing either one of those options were bad to him. He has the right to enjoy his life through the desires, and would make him a pack-ass for carrying a burden by creating a budget. Arkad explained that the things we not need to survive are desires and don't need, and we should think carefully about what the difference is. For each man there were all equally poor but there was a variety of incomes and family sizes.

The third cure

This chapter speaks well about how only having many pieces of gold only gratifies the holder but does nothing additionally. And the third step is to now have that money do its own labouring. Only then will the beholder accumulate real wealth. A gold coin can provide labour and bring in 2 gold coins as children, then you put those children to work and have them bring back more children continuously on repeat. But not all investments will work, you may lose everything just as Arkad did teh first time so be careful but more importantly don't be afraid to take risks and put the money to work.

The fourth cure

In the fourth chapter Arkad made it clear that the way to grow our wealth we need to make investments and also dived deeper into the mistake he made on his first investment he made. He explained that in order to make a good investment the background information of the person requesting the money should have a good reputation. He needs to be trustworthy, and the way we find that out is by speaking amongst others that have already made investments with the person in question. He also explained that misfortune loves a shiny mark, and thus we should know how to protect our gold before the Gods entrust us with more. And also spoke that we shouldn't be afraid of taking risks by making investments, but be careful and do research on the individual.

The fifth cure

In this chapter Arkad really spoke about one thing, which was to buy your own home instead of renting buti think there is a deeper meaning behind this. My takeaway from that chapter was that it's better to make a purchase whether loan or not of something instead of paying rent and never really owning it. He specifically said that it's better take a loan to purchase a house and pay back the lender overtime, for every payment is bringing down the debt. Which will be best for the family's future

The sixth cure

In chapter six, Arkad speaks on how a man's days are numbered and those numbers are unknown. And after the Gods cut his life short and call him among them, their family might not be secured with wealth. A solution that he disagrees with is burying the gold in land somewhere for it might be found by thieves. Another problem is for a late man that is no longer young and no longer has the ability to work anymore. And his solution for both of these is to ensure that a well sum of money is within the position of the man, whether it be by using banks and growing interest on deposited money other means. But everyman should not be blinded by living in the present and should look towards the days to come. We should all make investments for our future selves so that we don't get forsaken.

The seventh cure

In this chapter, Arkad was telling us that we should acquire skills that give money and perfect those skills better than most of the competition. And we should be confident in ourselves that what we have learnt so far and that we are able to carry them out correctly. In terms today, I think it translates to being the best version of yourself, do not procrastinate, limit mistakes and provide for everyone dear to you so that they shall speak greatness about me. "Thus the seventh and last remedy for a lean purse is to cultivate thy own powers, to study amd become wiser, to become more skillful, to so act as to respect thyself" .

The 5 Laws of Gold

This story is about the son of Arkad's journey to becoming a successful man. Arkad's son, was sent on a ten-year quest to a city called nineveth to accumilate a wealthy fortune by using the wisdom of his father's table. That table had the 5 laws of gold that he was instructed to follow and live by.

The first law

The first law explains that our fortune shall increase as long as we use only 9/10 of our direct income. If we always put one-tenth of our income of gold then we, in the future, shall have an estate for us and our family.

The second law

The second law is that Gold is a reliable slave and is eager to multiply for the wise man as long as he puts it to work. In time, it shall multiply in surprising fashion.

The third law

The third law tells us that gold sticks to the cautious owner, the man that is cautious about his gold and invests it wisely by knowing the investment or consulting another man wise in investments familiar with the specific investment.

The fourth law

This law tells us that if we are not wise in choosing the type of investment then we shall not intend to see that gold again. For the one who invests in something that they aren't familiar with or the skilled didn't approve wil have their gold slippeth away from.

The fifth law

The fifth law tells is that we should not force our money in impossible earnings and to not be tricked by alluring advice from tricksters. This is backed up by law four to keep our gold in investments that we understand and is familiar with.

Reflection of "The Richest Man in Babylon"

As a technology student preparing for the future, reading George S. Clason's "The Richest Man in Babylon" ties to my life and gives me a blueprint for the traps to look out for in the future. The chapter "7 Cures for a Lean Purse" really aligns with my goal of getting into a position to eventually start a family. The advice to "increase thy ability to earn" correlates with my tech major, as my current actions are the investments I make for my own future. The first cure, "saving at least ten percent of everything earned" gives a guide to build the wealth needed to support a household long before those major life milestones arrive.

"The 5 Laws of Gold," gave a similar overlap of Clason's principles and my personal relationship with money.While I do not struggle with reckless spending, I have a strong preference for purchasing high quality items, which is naturally expensive. The book's speaks about controlling expenditures and distinguishing between needs and casual wants and validates my approach, but it also serves as a gentle reminder to make sure that quality doesn't make me a slave to lifestyle inflation.

In conclusion, financial security is achievable through discipline and knowledge. It doesn't happen by accident. Receiving a fortune means very little without the proper wisdom required to sustain it.
