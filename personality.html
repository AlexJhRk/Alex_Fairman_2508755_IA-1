LLMs such as ChatGPT, Claude, and CoPilot must be used as a tool not a crutch

Large Language Models (LLMs) are types of Artificial Intelligence (AI) that can both understand and create human language, do tasks and research. In the world we live in today LLMs are now more popular than ever, being one of the fastest-growing technologies in history and ChatGPT alone being used by approximately 900 Million users weekly. (LLMs Statistics, 2025) Being a student through this worldwide breakthrough of technology is amazing with lots of benefits, LLMs should be used as a tool and not as a crutch.

"LLMs should be used as a tool and not as a crutch" What does this statement mean to me? It means that though these LLMs can make our life easier with information and carrying out operations, we should not be totally dependent on these LLMs and should still be able to carry out those tasks without them. Within my school, there is one side of students are very dependent on LLMs instead of actually learning where on the other side there are students that mainly use the LLMs to help them learn faster. A March 2025 study by researchers Mathias Lehmann, Phillip B. Cornelius, and Fabia J. String reveals that LLMs can either significantly boost or sseverely harm learning, depending entirely on how they are used.



How to Use LLMs

How to Enhance Learning

Use the LLM as a personal tutor. Conversing about the topic, asking for deep explanations, and seeking guidance actively improves learning outcomes. This approach is highly effective for students who lack prior domain knowledge. (Standford, 2025)

How to Impair Learning

Use the LLM as a shortcut. Excessively relying on the tool to solve practice exercises directly replaces a student's own mental effort, halting actual comprehension. First-time AI users are particularly prone to this trap.(Standford, 2025)


The Psychological Pitfall

The study warns against the "illusion of competence." The self-perceived benefits of using an LLM often exceed the actual benefits. Because the AI makes generating answers feel effortless, students easily overestimate their standalone abilities.
Ultimately, LLMs hold immense promise as learning aids, but students must be deeply cautious not to automate the cognitive struggle required to learn.
